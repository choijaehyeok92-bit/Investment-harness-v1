# Investment Philosophy — Long-term Outlier Expectation-Gap Strategy v2.0

## One-sentence definition

Invest for the long term in companies where the market underestimates the scale and duration of economic-value growth, where competitive advantage strengthens over time, where incremental capital can be reinvested at high returns, and where successful outcomes offer materially greater upside than the permanent-loss downside.

Long holding periods are conditional on evidence. Capital should be added to winners as business evidence strengthens, and the thesis must change when business evidence deteriorates regardless of share price.

## Five principles

### 1. Buy growth in economic value, not growth rate alone

Recognized growth should ultimately support growth in per-share FCF and per-share economic value.

Do not reward growth that depends structurally on:

- very high incremental capital needs
- persistent equity issuance or dilution
- subsidies, aggressive discounting, or excessive marketing
- worsening unit economics
- cyclical peaks misidentified as structural growth
- large TAM claims without a credible cash-flow path

Primary question:

> Not “How fast can it grow?” but “How much larger and for how much longer can it grow than the market expects?”

### 2. Buy expectation gaps, not merely good companies

`Expectation Gap = estimated long-term economic value - expectations embedded in current price`

A high-quality company can still be a poor investment if current price requires near-perfect success.

### 3. Evaluate moat trajectory

Focus on whether competitive advantage is strengthening.

Observe:

- market-share direction
- retention
- usage per customer
- pricing power
- customer-acquisition economics
- unit-cost direction
- data/network effects
- ecosystem growth
- R&D productivity
- cross-selling

### 4. Power-law outcomes matter

The objective is not maximum hit rate. The objective is to survive failures while retaining enough exposure to rare 10x/30x/100x winners.

### 5. React to evidence, not price

Price declines do not prove a thesis wrong. Price increases do not prove it right.
Quarterly misses alone do not invalidate a long-term thesis.
“Long term” is not permission to ignore structural deterioration.

## Conceptual expected-alpha model

`Expected Alpha ≈ Business Outlier Quality × Expectation Gap × Duration × Asymmetry`

This is a decision framework, not a literal forecasting equation.

## Portfolio principle

Target roughly 20–30 potential outlier candidates across stages of evidence maturity.

`Diversified enough to find the winner, concentrated enough to benefit from it.`

## Macro principle

Macro is not a stock-selection engine.

`Macro ≠ Stock Selection`

`Macro = Risk Budget / Position Pacing`

Use macro conditions to adjust buying pace, liquidity reserves, and portfolio risk budget—not the company-quality score.
