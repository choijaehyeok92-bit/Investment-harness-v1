# Red Team — TSM

As of 2026-09-04 · per `agents/red-team.md`
This document does not recommend a purchase and does not defend the thesis.

---

## Strongest bear thesis

**You are being paid a 1.5% free-cash-flow yield to own a business that must spend a
third of its revenue every year, forever, on an island that a nuclear power claims.**

Strip the quality narrative and the arithmetic is uncomfortable. TSMC earned
US$121.4B of revenue in 2025 and converted it into **US$31.6B of free cash flow** —
because US$40.9B went straight back into capex, and FY2026 guidance takes that to
**US$52–56B**. Against a US$2,147B market capitalisation that is a **1.47% trailing
FCF yield** and a **39.7x trailing P/E**. Dividends add 0.85%. There are no buybacks.

The bull answer is "look forward, not trailing" — but the forward number rests on the
analyst's *own* 47% net-margin assumption for a company that **does not guide
margin**. Change that one input to 2025's actual 44.6% and the forward P/E moves from
26.8x to 28.3x. **The single most important valuation input in this file is an
assumption the analyst invented.**

And the capex is not optional. A foundry that stops spending stops being a foundry.

## Attacks required by the contract

**1. What if the apparent structural growth is cyclical?**
This is my weakest attack and I concede most of it. Margins *expanding* through 76%
volume growth is not cycle behaviour. But note the mix: **HPC went 43% → 58% of
revenue in two years.** TSMC has become a leveraged play on one end market whose
customers fund purchases from balance sheet, not operating cash flow. The company
diversified *away* from smartphones into something more concentrated, not less.

**2. What if TAM growth does not translate into company cash flow?**
**It demonstrably does not, and this is the core attack.** Revenue +76% over two
years; free cash flow went from roughly US$9.4B (2023) to US$31.6B (2025) — better,
but on a base where a third of every revenue dollar is permanently reinvested. TSMC
enables perhaps US$400B+ of downstream AI value and captures a 59.9% gross margin on
the wafer while NVIDIA captures 75% on the chip. **TSMC is the utility; its customers
are the landlords.**

**3. What if moat expansion is being confused with revenue growth?**
Here the analyst is right and I cannot land the attack. Margin expansion during
volume growth is genuine moat evidence — the cleanest in this harness. I withdraw it.

**4. What if customer value is overstated?**
59.9% gross margin says it is not. Conceded.

**5. What if incremental ROIC is already deteriorating?**
Not yet — margins up 8.2pp at flat capital intensity is rising incremental ROIC. **But
the depreciation from US$52–56B of 2026 capex lands in 2027–28**, and D&A was already
NT$688,096M in 2025. The margin peak may be a 2026 event. The analyst scores this
INVESTIGATE at MEDIUM; on the depreciation-wall arithmetic alone HIGH is defensible.

**6. What if FCF/share growth is overstated by SBC or dilution?**
No dilution, but **no buybacks either.** Share count is flat at 25.93bn. Per-share
value creation depends entirely on earnings growth, with a 0.85% dividend. Both
005930 and NVDA are *retiring* stock. This is the category where TSMC is genuinely
worst, and the analyst scores it 12/15 — which I think is a point too generous.

**7. What if management capital allocation contradicts the stated strategy?**
It does not, and the record is excellent. My one flag: the **"irrevocable, absolute
and unconditional guarantee"** to the US Department of Commerce is full parent
recourse on a subsidiary's obligations, accepted in exchange for subsidy — with
conditions restricting expansion in "foreign countries of concern." **TSMC has
accepted a constraint on its own strategic freedom from a foreign government.** For a
company whose central risk is geopolitical, that is not a trivial trade.

**8. What if the market already understands the bullish thesis?**
Largely, yes — but here the analyst has the better argument. The persistent discount
to US-listed AI peers *is* the market pricing the Taiwan risk. The investment question
is whether the discount over- or under-compensates, and that is a genuine judgement
rather than an informational edge. At least the file says so.

**9. Which assumption contributes the most to valuation?**
Two, and they compound: the **47% FY2026 net margin** (invented by the analyst) and
the **5% Taiwan contingency weight** (unknowable by construction). The file discloses
both. Disclosure is not robustness.

**10. What single event could create permanent loss?**
A Taiwan contingency. The analyst and I agree, and agree it is unresearchable.

## Three most dangerous hidden assumptions

1. **That the 2027–28 depreciation wall is absorbable.** US$52–56B of 2026 capex
   depreciates into 2027–28 income statements. The base case assumes 45% net margins
   hold through that. Nothing in the file tests it.
2. **That overseas fabs meaningfully de-risk Taiwan.** The analyst's own increase
   trigger assumes they will. But Arizona, Kumamoto and Dresden are years behind at
   leading edge and structurally higher-cost. **They may diversify revenue without
   diversifying the risk that matters.**
3. **That a 3–7% five-year contingency probability is the right order of magnitude.**
   It is a number with no evidentiary basis. It could be 1%. It could be 15%. The
   position sizing rests on it.

## Evidence the analyst underweighted

- **The depreciation wall.** Mentioned as counter-evidence but not modelled. It is the
  most predictable margin headwind TSMC faces and it arrives inside the forecast period.
- **The absence of buybacks.** Treated as a category deduction; it deserves more. At a
  1.47% FCF yield and 0.85% dividend, an investor's entire return depends on multiple
  and earnings — there is **no cash-return floor**.
- **The customer-mix irony.** Diversifying from smartphones into HPC increased
  end-market concentration. The file records both facts but does not connect them.

## Red-team confidence

**0.55** — the lowest I have assigned in this harness, and deliberately so. The
business quality is real and I could not break it on the operating evidence. My
attacks land on **valuation arithmetic** and on an **unknowable tail**, neither of
which is falsifiable in the direction I would need. I am arguing about price and
probability, not about whether this is a great company. It plainly is.

## Verdict: **PASS** — with two dissents recorded

The thesis survives, and it survives well. It scores the business honestly (66/75,
highest in this harness), it passes `low_quality_growth` and `moat_shrinkage` on
evidence rather than assertion, it labels its own FY2026 margin figure an ESTIMATE and
gives the sensitivity, it models the Taiwan contingency as a separate tail rather than
burying it in the bear case, and it states plainly that the dominant gate is
unresearchable.

**Dissent 1 — scoring.** `incremental_roic_and_fcf_per_share` at 12/15 is a point too
generous given 33% capex intensity, a 1.47% FCF yield, no buybacks, and an unmodelled
2027–28 depreciation wall. At 11/15 the total is 78, which changes nothing — so this
is a quibble, and I record it as one.

**Dissent 2 — sizing logic, and this one matters.** The analyst argues that because
the Taiwan gate is unresearchable, waiting adds nothing, so size small and hold.
**That logic is sound but it cuts both ways:** if the dominant risk cannot be reduced
by work, then it also cannot be reduced by conviction, and the position should be
sized off the *tail*, not off the business quality. A 1–2% STARTER happens to be
consistent with both readings — which is why I do not dissent from the conclusion,
only from how confidently it is reached.

**Required before any increase beyond STARTER:** model the 2027–28 depreciation
impact on margins explicitly, and establish overseas leading-edge capacity as a share
of total. The first is arithmetic the file has not done. The second is the only
increase trigger that touches the risk that actually matters.
