## The headline: the screening run was wrong about what this company is

The 2026-09-04 screening run ranked 012450 **fifth for deep work** as a European
rearmament play *"uncorrelated with AI capex."* The filings say otherwise.

| H1 2026 segment | Revenue | Share | Operating profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **해양 (Marine)** | **KRW 9,614.2B** | **63.91%** | **KRW 1,150.3B** |
| 방산 (Defence) | KRW 4,465.2B | 29.68% | KRW 903.7B |
| 항공 (Aviation) | KRW 1,459.0B | 9.70% | KRW 67.2B |
| IT서비스 / 항공우주 | KRW 486.8B | 3.24% | KRW 24.7B |

**By revenue this is now majority a shipbuilding company** — and marine has overtaken
defence on operating profit too. In FY2025 marine was 54.5% of revenue with
KRW 1,115.3B of operating profit against defence at 38.9% and KRW 2,272.6B. **The mix
has shifted decisively toward the lower-quality business in twelve months.**

Shipbuilding is a **cyclical** industry with a historically poor record on returns on
capital — not a structural-growth industry. Correcting this misidentification is the
main work of this analysis.

## 1. Structural change and market leadership — 10/15

**What is real:** order backlog of **KRW 114.92T** — about **3.8 years** of revenue at
the H1 2026 annualised rate. Defence backlog alone is **KRW 46.85T** (한화에어로
KRW 38.31T + 한화시스템 KRW 8.54T); 항공 KRW 31.72T; 해양 KRW 33.01T. European
rearmament is a treaty- and budget-driven shift measured in decades, and Korea's edge
is deliverable capacity while European primes are constrained. That case survives.

**What is not:** the mix problem above, plus **defence revenue is shrinking** —
KRW 4,465.2B in H1 2026 against an FY2025 half-year run-rate of KRW 5,191.6B, about
**−14%**.

And a measurement warning: the FY2024→FY2025 revenue jump from KRW 11.24T to
KRW 26.70T (+137%) is **not organic growth** — it reflects the **consolidation of
Hanwha Ocean**. Any comparison spanning that boundary is meaningless.

## 2. Customer value and product strength — 7/10

Defence operating margin of **20.2%** (H1 2026) and 21.9% (FY2025) is high for weapons
systems and reflects genuine product value in K9 howitzers, Chunmoo launchers and
related systems. Marine margin improved from **7.7% → 12.0%**.

Against: demand is a **government-budget derivative** with sovereign negotiating power
and offset obligations on exports; shipbuilding customer value is structurally low
(largely commoditised, fixed-price); and **aviation posted a pre-tax loss** of
KRW 19.9B on a 4.6% operating margin.

## 3. Moat trajectory — 8/15

**The moat leg is deteriorating on both lines.** Defence revenue **−14%** *and* defence
margin **21.9% → 20.2%**, while the lower-moat marine business grows. Mix went marine
54.5% → 63.9% and defence 38.9% → 29.7% in twelve months.

`agents/deep-analyst.md` demands direction-of-change evidence. **The direction on the
part of the business that carries the moat is negative.**

Against that: the KRW 114.9T backlog is real, marine margin doubling is genuine
operational improvement, and European local-production JVs plus the US naval MRO and
Philly Shipyard positions are attempts to convert export relationships into embedded
ones — though local-production requirements structurally lower margin over time.

## 4. Incremental ROIC and FCF/share — 5/15

**The decisive category, and the lowest score assigned in this harness.**

| H1 | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating profit | **KRW 2,004.4B** | — |
| **Operating cash flow** | **KRW −370.7B** | **KRW +1,014.3B** |
| Cash from operations (pre-interest/tax) | KRW −139.8B | KRW +1,579.8B |
| Investing cash flow | KRW −3,403.0B | KRW −952.0B |
| Interest paid | KRW 264.7B | KRW 232.9B |

**KRW 2.00T of operating profit produced NEGATIVE KRW 370.7B of operating cash flow** —
a KRW 1.39T year-on-year swing. Borrowings rose **KRW 12.39T → KRW 15.44T (+24.6%)** in
six months to fund the gap. **Interest was paid out of financing, not operations.**

And this follows a **13.1% dilution in 2025**: 5,982,240 new shares (1,715,040 by
third-party placement on 2025-04-29; 4,267,200 by rights issue on 2025-07-10), taking
the count from 45,581,161 to 51,563,401. **Equity raised, then debt raised, while
operations consumed cash.**

Finally, **non-controlling interests take about 44% of profit** — H1 2026 continuing
profit KRW 1,606.8B versus owners' share KRW 897.1B. Consolidated figures materially
overstate what reaches Hanwha Aerospace shareholders.

*In fairness:* long-cycle defence and shipbuilding contracts accumulate contract assets
before milestone billing, so a single negative half-year **can** be timing. That is why
this scores 5 rather than 2 — and why FY2025 full-year operating cash flow is the
single most important missing number in this file.

## 5. Management and capital allocation — 5/10

Strategic expansion is being executed, and retained earnings are being retained
(KRW 4.75T → 5.98T → 6.50T). But the 2025 raise diluted holders 13.1%, borrowings still
rose 24.6% in the six months *after* it, and **capital surplus rose a further KRW 1.42T
in H1 2026 with no new shares issued** — implying transactions in subsidiary interests
whose nature is not established in the material reviewed.

## 6. Financial survivability — 6/10

Cash KRW 8,048.2B and total equity KRW 20,237.1B (owners' KRW 11,998.9B). Against that:
net debt **≈ KRW 7.40T and rising**, and a **current ratio of only 1.08**
(current assets KRW 32,904.3B vs current liabilities KRW 30,493.1B).

**The weakest survivability profile in this harness.** Samsung, NVDA and TSM hold net
cash; AVGO is levered but generates large free cash flow. This company is levered
**and** cash-consuming.

**Business-quality subtotal (§1–6): 41/75 — far below every other name analysed**
(TSM 66, NVDA 62, 005930 59, AVGO 57).

## 7. Key risks and concentration dependencies

Demand is a **government-budget derivative** on both legs. Segment concentration is
worsening toward the more cyclical, lower-return business. Leverage, negative cash
generation and a cyclical revenue majority **coincide** — none of the other four names
has all three.

*Mitigant:* minorities absorb ~44% of profit, so the parent's economic exposure to
marine is smaller than consolidated revenue implies.

## Why the price makes this a REJECT

| At KRW 1,244,000 | |
|---|---|
| Market capitalisation | **KRW 64.14T** (51,563,401 shares) |
| Owners' equity / BPS | KRW 12.00T / **KRW 232,703** |
| **Price-to-book** | **5.35x** |
| Trailing P/E (FY2025 owners' EPS KRW 27,247) | **45.7x** |
| P/E on H1 2026 annualised (KRW 34,796) | **35.8x** |

**Reverse-engineered:** even at a generous **22x** the price requires owners' net income
to rise about **60%** from the annualised KRW 1.79T and *stay* there. At a more normal
**15–18x** for a business with a cyclical revenue majority it requires a **doubling or
more**.

| Scenario | 5-yr total return |
|---|---|
| Bear (30%) | **−81% to −72%** |
| Base (50%) | **−44% to −30%** |
| **Bull (20%)** | **+9% to +40%** |
| **Probability-weighted** | **≈ −37%** (−8.7%/yr) |

**The bull case barely beats cash.** That is the clearest statement of how demanding
this price is. And the conclusion is **robust to reweighting** — even at bear 0.15 /
base 0.45 / bull 0.40 the weighted return stays around **−18%**.

**REJECT is a statement about the investment at this price, not about the company.**
The backlog is real; the defence structural driver is real. But this strategy exists to
find asymmetric outliers, and a distribution whose optimistic case barely beats cash
while the downside is levered is the opposite of that.

**Note:** no Hard Veto reads FAIL. The label follows the score (47/100, Reject band)
and the negative expected return — not a veto trigger.

## Most important unknowns

1. **FY2025 full-year and H2 2026 operating cash flow.** The single most important
   missing number. It decides whether negative H1 2026 OCF is contract-asset timing or
   a structural condition — and whether `external_capital_dependence` becomes a FAIL.
2. Whether the defence revenue decline is delivery-schedule timing or order intake.
3. The KRW 1.42T H1 2026 capital surplus increase with no new shares.
4. What the March 2026 correction to the annual report changed.
5. Use of proceeds from the 2025 raise, and whether a further raise is contemplated.

## Evidence that would strengthen the thesis

- Operating cash flow turning clearly positive for FY2026
- Defence revenue and margin re-accelerating on order intake, not just backlog
- Borrowings stabilising below KRW 15.44T without a further equity raise
- Marine margin sustaining ≥12% through a full cycle
- **A price decline of roughly 40–50%** — but only alongside the cash-flow evidence
  above, since `policy/position-sizing.yaml` never permits acting on price alone

## Evidence that would weaken the thesis further

- FY2026 operating cash flow negative for the full year → `external_capital_dependence`
  moves to **FAIL** and REJECT becomes veto-driven rather than score-driven
- Any further equity raise twelve months after the last
- Defence **backlog** declining, not just revenue
- Marine margin reverting toward 7.7%
- Borrowings above ~KRW 18T, or current ratio below 1.0
