## Strongest case AGAINST the analyst's REJECT

**The analyst may be convicting a long-cycle contractor on a working-capital quarter.**

Defence and shipbuilding accumulate **contract assets** as work-in-progress before
milestone billing. Contract assets rose KRW 6,487.7B → **KRW 7,065.5B** in the half.
Inventory *fell*. Receivables rose modestly. That is exactly what a business ramping
deliveries against a KRW 114.9T backlog looks like — cash goes out before it comes in.

**The analyst has one half-year of cash flow data and no full-year comparison**, and
says so. Convicting on that is a real risk. H1 2025 OCF was +KRW 1,014.3B, which
*proves* this business can generate cash — so the negative half is a deviation from its
own recent record, not a demonstrated norm.

And the backlog is genuinely extraordinary: **3.8 years of revenue**, with defence
alone at KRW 46.85T. Very few industrials anywhere have that visibility.

**If FY2026 full-year OCF comes in at, say, +KRW 1.5T, most of this analysis collapses**
and the score should rise materially.

## Attacks required by the contract

**1. What if the apparent structural growth is cyclical?**
The analyst's own point, and I agree — but note it cuts *both* ways. Marine at 12.0%
margin is likely near a cyclical peak. **The base case assumes marine normalises;
the bear assumes it turns. Neither models marine staying at 12% for years**, which is
what a genuine MASGA/US-naval re-shoring would produce.

**2. What if TAM growth does not translate into company cash flow?**
It demonstrably has not — this half. That is the analyst's strongest fact and I cannot
break it. **But "has not for six months" is not "does not."**

**3. What if moat expansion is being confused with revenue growth?**
Fully conceded and well handled. Defence revenue −14% with margin 21.9%→20.2% while
the mix shifts to marine is the correct read.

**4. What if customer value is overstated?**
A 20.2% defence margin says it is not. But note the analyst under-weights the
**offset obligation** problem: European local-production requirements are how these
orders are won, and they structurally transfer value to the customer country. The
thesis mentions it once; it deserves more.

**5. What if incremental ROIC is already deteriorating?**
Yes — and it is worse than the file states, because **44% of profit goes to minorities**
while 100% of the debt sits on the consolidated balance sheet. Returns to the *parent*
are lower than the consolidated numbers suggest on both the numerator and the leverage.

**6. What if FCF/share growth is overstated by SBC or dilution?**
The 13.1% dilution is confirmed and quantified. The analyst is **fair** to note the
count has been flat for twelve months — I would have led with the dilution and buried
that mitigant.

**7. What if management capital allocation contradicts the stated strategy?**
Raising equity in April and July 2025, then adding **KRW 3.05T of debt** in H1 2026, is
a company funding itself externally twice within eighteen months. The unexplained
**KRW 1.42T capital surplus increase with no share issuance** is the loosest thread in
the file and should have been chased into note 25 before publishing.

**8. What if the market already understands the bearish thesis?**
**MY STRONGEST ATTACK ON THE REJECT.** The market pays 5.35x book and 35.8x earnings.
That is not a market ignoring cash flow — it is a market pricing the KRW 114.9T backlog
and a decade of European rearmament. **The analyst is not finding something the market
missed; it is disagreeing with the market's discount rate.** That is a legitimate
position, but it is a *valuation opinion*, not an informational edge, and the file
should say so plainly.

**9. Which assumption contributes the most?**
The base case terminal P/E of 18x on owners' net income of KRW 2.0–2.5T. At 25x and
KRW 3.0T — not heroic given the backlog — equity is KRW 75T and the base return turns
**positive**. The REJECT rests on a normalisation assumption the file cannot yet prove.

**10. What single event could create permanent loss?**
A marine downturn while OCF is negative and net debt is rising, forcing a dilutive
recapitalisation. I accept 8–12%.

## Three most dangerous hidden assumptions

1. **That negative H1 OCF is representative.** One half-year, against a positive prior
   half, in a business whose accounting is dominated by percentage-of-completion.
2. **That marine at 12% margin must normalise down.** If US naval MRO and Philly
   Shipyard are structural, marine may be *re-rating*, not peaking — the file never
   models that.
3. **That 5.35x book is expensive.** For a business whose book value is understated by
   a KRW 114.9T off-balance-sheet backlog, book value may be the wrong denominator
   entirely. The analyst uses it as a headline anchor without testing that.

# Red Team addendum — corrections applied, 2026-09-04

The analyst checked my REVISE and **one of my two grounds was factually wrong.**

Net debt/EBITDA was **1.6x at 2025-09-30** with coverage rated excellent, implying
roughly **1.2–1.3x** on the current run-rate. **That is conservative leverage by any
conventional standard.** The earlier draft's "weakest survivability in the harness"
framing was too harsh on the debt *level*; `financial_survivability` is correctly

The genuine concern is narrower and I should have stated it that way from the start:
**not solvency or debt quantum, but cash conversion** — a 1.08 current ratio and
negative operating cash flow while borrowings rose 24.6% in six months.
