## Executive thesis

Intuitive Surgical is the category-defining leader in robotic-assisted soft-tissue surgery. Its economic engine is not primarily the sale of robots: a growing installed base drives recurring instruments, accessories, service and operating-lease revenue each time customers perform procedures. At June 30, 2026, the installed base included approximately 11,710 da Vinci systems and 1,096 Ion systems. Recurring revenue represented 85% of Q2 revenue. Surgeon training, hospital workflows, clinical evidence, regulatory approvals, manufacturing know-how, service infrastructure and more than 5,600 active patents reinforce one another and make the ecosystem difficult to replicate.

Operating evidence remains excellent. Q2 2026 revenue increased 19% to $2.892 billion; da Vinci procedures increased 15% to approximately 889,000; Ion procedures increased 36% to approximately 47,900; and da Vinci system utilization increased 3%. Da Vinci's installed base grew 12%, while the Ion installed base grew 21%. Q2 operating income rose 31%, faster than revenue, and calculated trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was approximately $3.223 billion, or 29.2% of revenue. Period-end shares declined approximately 1.3% year over year because repurchases more than offset employee equity issuance.

The business therefore passes the quality screen with a wide and still-strengthening moat. The investment question is price. At $365.56, the equity-value proxy is approximately $129.4 billion, or 11.7x trailing revenue, 40.1x reported FCF and 41.9x diluted EPS. A ten-year reverse DCF requires approximately 14.1% annual revenue growth if year-ten FCF margin reaches 30%, or 13.4% if the margin reaches 32%. Those assumptions are possible, but they require the current high-growth phase to persist for unusually long despite procedure growth already decelerating from 18% in 2025 to 15% in H1 2026.

The correct decision is **WATCH**. Intuitive is an exceptional business and a credible long-duration compounder, but the reference price is approximately equal to a defensible Base value rather than materially below it. The company should enter the portfolio only after either a sufficient valuation gap opens or new evidence raises the defensible long-run growth and margin range.

## Screener result - SCREEN_IN

### Top positive signals

1. Procedure volume, installed base and utilization are all increasing, creating a reinforcing installed-base and recurring-consumables model.
2. Recurring revenue is 85% of revenue, and Q2 operating income grew materially faster than revenue.
3. The company has $8.63 billion of cash and investments, no meaningful financial-debt burden, positive FCF and net and is reducing net shares.

### Top uncertainties

1. Whether da Vinci procedure growth can remain in the mid-teens for another decade as U.S. penetration matures.
2. Whether competitive systems from Medtronic, Johnson & Johnson, CMR Surgical and Chinese manufacturers weaken pricing, placements or utilization.
3. Whether the economic value of robotic assistance remains compelling in high-volume benign procedures where hospitals are more cost sensitive.

### Obvious veto flags

No obvious veto fails. High valuation, legal exposure, product-safety risk and dependence on the da Vinci ecosystem require monitoring but do not currently constitute a Hard Veto.

## 1. Structural change and market leadership - 14/15

**Fact:** Robotic-assisted minimally invasive surgery continues to expand by procedure and geography. In 2025, da Vinci procedures grew 18% to approximately 3.153 million and Ion procedures grew 51% to approximately 144,100. In Q2 2026, da Vinci procedures grew 15% and Ion procedures grew 36%.

**Fact:** Intuitive offers more than 70 representative da Vinci clinical uses across general surgery, urology, gynecology, cardiothoracic surgery and head and neck surgery. Da Vinci 5, the fifth-generation multi-port platform, reached an installed base of 1,710 systems by June 2026, while da Vinci SP and Ion create adjacent growth vectors.

**Inference:** Intuitive is not merely benefiting from the shift toward robotic-assisted care; it has defined the category through systems, instruments, training, service and clinical-development infrastructure. International adoption, new procedure clearances and the replacement cycle from Xi to da Vinci 5 can extend category growth.

**Counter-evidence:** Adoption is not automatic. It depends on procedure-specific clinical value, hospital economics, surgeon acceptance, reimbursement and regulatory clearance. More than half of da Vinci procedures address benign conditions, where hospitals are more sensitive to treatment cost.

## 2. Customer value and product strength - 9/10

**Fact:** Intuitive frames customer value across patients, physicians and hospitals: less invasive procedures, surgeon ergonomics and precision, and potential reductions in complications and length of stay. Q2 da Vinci utilization increased 3%, indicating that procedure growth was not driven only by new placements.

**Fact:** The company generally earns approximately $900-$3,700 of instruments and accessories revenue per da Vinci procedure and annual service fees of approximately $95,000-$225,000 per system. The da Vinci 5 platform adds force-feedback instruments and My Intuitive+ digital capabilities.

**Inference:** Repeated use by trained surgeons and integrated operating-room teams is stronger evidence of customer value than placements alone. The combination of procedural know-how, reliable service and consumable availability creates meaningful switching friction.

**Counter-evidence:** Benefits vary by procedure and patient. Hospitals facing staffing, budget and reimbursement pressure may prefer conventional laparoscopy or lower-priced robotic systems. Third-party remanufactured instruments and unauthorized service can also lower customer costs while pressuring Intuitive's recurring revenue.

## 3. Moat trajectory - 14/15

**Fact:** The da Vinci installed base increased 12% year over year to approximately 11,710 systems; the Ion base increased 21% to approximately 1,096. Recurring revenue remained 85% of revenue in Q2 2026. Intuitive owned more than 5,600 active granted patents and more than 2,500 pending patents at year-end 2025.

**Inference:** The moat is widening through a flywheel: more systems enable more procedures; more procedures generate clinical data, trained users and recurring revenue; those resources fund R&D, regulatory approvals, manufacturing and customer support; the broader ecosystem makes replacement more costly and risky.

**Counter-evidence:** System leasing lowers initial adoption friction but can also make competitive switching easier. Large medical-device competitors have capital, hospital relationships and complementary product portfolios. Chinese domestic competitors are already affecting placements, and certain legacy patents will expire over time.

## 4. Incremental ROIC and FCF/share economics - 13/15

**Fact:** H1 2026 revenue increased 21% to $5.663 billion, while operating income increased 38% to $1.827 billion. Calculated TTM operating income was approximately $3.451 billion, or 31.3% of revenue.

**Fact:** Calculated TTM operating cash flow was approximately $3.706 billion. After approximately $484 million of capital expenditures, calculated TTM FCF was $3.223 billion, or 29.2% of revenue.

**Fact:** Calculated TTM share-based compensation expense was approximately $839 million, equal to 7.6% of revenue and 26.0% of reported FCF. Period-end shares declined from 358.4 million in June 2025 to 353.9 million in June 2026. H1 repurchases totaled $1.507 billion at an average price of $475.67, materially above the reference price.

**Inference:** Procedure-driven consumables and service revenue generate attractive incremental returns, and repurchases currently offset dilution. Owner economics are substantially cleaner than at many technology companies, although FCF should still be considered after SBC and the capital required for factories, lease assets and inventory.

**Counter-evidence:** Inventory rose to $2.029 billion, and $515 million of H1 operating cash was absorbed by inventory growth. Increasing use of operating leases shifts cash recovery later and raises asset intensity. Large repurchases at prices well above the current market price demonstrate imperfect valuation discipline.

## 5. Management and capital allocation - 8/10

**Fact:** David Rosa became CEO on July 1, 2025 after a long internal career, while former CEO Gary Guthart became Executive Chair. Eight of ten director nominees were independent, all board committees were independent, and the company has one common-share class with equal voting rights.

**Fact:** In H1 2026, Intuitive acquired former distributors serving Italy, Spain, Portugal and related territories for approximately $533 million, bringing those markets under direct distribution. The company also authorized $5.0 billion for repurchases in April 2026, with approximately $4.7 billion remaining at June 30.

**Fact:** The 2026 proxy proposed adding 5.0 million shares, approximately 1.4% of outstanding shares, to the equity incentive plan. Executive officers and directors beneficially owned approximately 0.6% of shares at year-end 2025. The July 2026 bylaw amendments increased procedural and disclosure requirements for shareholder nominations and proposals.

**Inference:** The internal CEO succession preserves culture and operating continuity, while direct distribution can improve customer intimacy and long-term economics. Governance is generally shareholder-friendly, but low insider ownership, equity-plan expansion, expensive repurchases and stricter nomination procedures warrant a deduction.

**Counter-evidence:** The distribution acquisition was not financially material, so its return is unproven. The leadership transition is recent, and the combination of an Executive Chair who previously served as CEO with a new CEO should be monitored for clear accountability.

## 6. Financial survivability - 10/10

**Fact:** At June 30, 2026, cash, cash equivalents and investments totaled approximately $8.63 billion. Total liabilities were $2.58 billion against $20.88 billion of assets, and the company disclosed no meaningful financial debt.

**Fact:** H1 operating cash flow was $1.973 billion, compared with $1.297 billion a year earlier. Management stated that current liquidity and internally generated cash should be sufficient for the foreseeable future.

**Inference:** External-capital dependence and solvency risk are very low. The balance sheet can absorb normal product cycles, regulatory delays, litigation and continued investment.

**Counter-evidence:** Leasing, manufacturing capacity, inventory and direct-distribution expansion increase cash needs. A severe safety event or broad procedure disruption could reduce cash generation while creating legal and remediation costs.

## 8. Power law and asymmetry - 5/10

Intuitive retains a credible path to become a much larger company if robotic assistance expands across global soft-tissue surgery, da Vinci 5 deepens platform economics, SP extends access to narrow workspaces, and Ion becomes a major lung-biopsy and interventional platform.

At the reference price, however, this business power law does not translate into strong stock asymmetry. Modeled Bear downside and Bull upside are both approximately 47%. A severe permanent-impairment case is approximately $100-$150 per share if procedure growth stalls, FCF margin compresses toward the high teens and the moat is impaired by safety, competition or reimbursement pressure. Subjective probability-weighted value is approximately $351 per share, about 4% below the reference price.

## Key risks and concentration dependencies

1. **Platform concentration:** most economics still depend on the da Vinci ecosystem and its recurring instruments and accessories.
2. **Patient safety and regulation:** product defects, recalls, field actions or inadequate training allegations could cause permanent reputational damage.
3. **Competition:** Medtronic, Johnson & Johnson, CMR Surgical, other robotic entrants and Chinese domestic systems may pressure placements and pricing.
4. **Recurring-revenue challenge:** remanufactured instruments, unauthorized service and antitrust litigation could weaken aftermarket economics.
5. **Hospital economics:** capital budgets, staffing, reimbursement and cost sensitivity can slow placements and procedure adoption.
6. **China:** domestic competition, procurement quotas, anti-corruption scrutiny, pricing limits and trade restrictions are already constraining placements.
7. **Supply chain:** sole- and single-source components, German endoscope production, Mexican manufacturing and Chinese rare-earth exposure create disruption and tariff risk.

## Most important unknowns

1. Independent market share by country and robotic-surgery procedure.
2. Procedure-level clinical and total-cost evidence for high-volume benign procedures.
3. Gross margin and utilization economics for leased da Vinci 5 and Ion systems.
4. Customer retention and competitive win/loss rates as new platforms enter hospitals.
5. Long-run procedure opportunity and sustainable growth after mature U.S. categories saturate.
6. Potential damages and conduct remedies from product-liability and EndoWrist aftermarket litigation.

## Thesis falsifiers

1. Da Vinci procedure growth remains below 10% for four consecutive quarters without an identifiable temporary cause.
2. Da Vinci installed-base growth falls below 7% while utilization also declines.
3. Instruments and accessories revenue grows materially slower than procedure volume for four quarters because of pricing, mix or remanufacturing.
4. A competitor wins sustained share in major U.S. or European health systems and causes net account losses or pricing pressure.
5. GAAP gross margin remains below 62% or FCF margin remains below 22% for two years.
6. Net share dilution exceeds 1.5% annually despite positive FCF and authorized repurchases.
7. A material product-safety event, recall or cybersecurity incident causes sustained procedure or placement declines.
8. Regulatory, reimbursement or antitrust remedies materially impair the limited-use instrument and service model.

## Evidence required before investment

- Da Vinci procedure growth stabilizes at 15% or higher while utilization remains positive.
- International procedure growth remains at least 18% and broadens beyond a few countries.
- Da Vinci 5 placements and procedure mix improve recurring revenue per installed system without reducing consolidated gross margin.
- Ion utilization accelerates after U.S. installed-base growth slows, demonstrating an economically attractive second platform.
- FCF per diluted share compounds at least 15% while net share count is flat or declining.
- Competitive launches do not cause material account losses, price concessions or reduced procedure growth.
- The market price falls at least 20% below a refreshed Base value, or stronger evidence raises Base value sufficiently to create that gap.

## Evidence that would justify reduction or exit

- Any thesis falsifier persists without a credible and measurable resolution path.
- A serious safety or regulatory event damages trust across the installed base.
- Procedure and utilization weakness occur together with competitor share gains.
- Management uses the balance sheet for low-return acquisitions or repurchases persistently above intrinsic value while per-share economics weaken.
- Price rises to a level requiring the full Bull case while business evidence does not improve proportionately.

## Conclusion

Intuitive Surgical is one of the highest-quality medical-technology franchises: it leads a structurally expanding category, generates 85% recurring revenue, converts growth into operating leverage and FCF, holds substantial net cash and currently offsets SBC with repurchases. The principal weakness is not company quality but the expectation gap. At $365.56, a defensible Base case is already reflected.

The final status is **WATCH / NONE**, with Hard Veto status **PASS**. A purchase requires evidence and valuation to improve together; a price decline by itself is not sufficient.
